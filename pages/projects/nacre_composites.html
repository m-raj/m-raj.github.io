Mechanical properties of nacre-like composites: a bottom-up approach

Mayank Raj, Sandeep P. Patil, Bernd Markert · IIT Madras & RWTH Aachen University
Journal of Composites Science 4 (2020) 35 · open access (CC BY)

Paper (DOI)

Nacre, or mother of pearl, is about 95% brittle aragonite, yet it is remarkably tough. The trick lies in its brick-and-mortar layout: stiff mineral tablets glued together by a thin layer of soft protein. Reported values for how stiff that protein "mortar" is range over two orders of magnitude, from 150 MPa to 15 GPa. This project first computes the stiffness of the protein layer from atoms up with molecular dynamics. It then feeds that value into 3D finite-element models to find how much mortar gives the toughest composite.

Two scales, one pipeline

Step 1: shearing the protein glue (molecular dynamics)

The organic matrix was built from the two proteins thought to matter most mechanically: Lustrin A (its elastic glycine–serine domain) and Perlucin. Two units of each were folded into U-shapes, packed between two aragonite slabs in a 16.1 × 41.1 × 16.1 nm³ water box, and simulated in GROMACS with the OPLS-AA force field. The bottom slab is held fixed and the top slab is dragged sideways at constant speed through a spring. The slope of shear stress against shear strain is the shear modulus.

Repeated simulations put the shear modulus at 1.25–1.45 GPa. That lands inside, and narrows, the very wide range reported in earlier experiments and models:

Step 2: the brick-and-mortar FE model

A unit hexagonal aragonite tablet wrapped in a thin organic layer is meshed, tiled across the plane, and extruded into three staggered lamellae. Widening the gaps between tablets and between layers changes the organic volume fraction from 5% to 40%. The model is fixed at one end and pulled at the other in LS-DYNA, an explicit solver.

Aragonite

E = 107 GPa, ν = 0.25. Brittle: cracks when the maximum principal stress reaches 160 MPa.

Organic matrix

Bilinear elastoplastic with isotropic hardening and no failure. E₀ = 3.78 GPa (from MD) or 11 GPa (from AFM, for comparison).

Validation

At 5% organic, the elastic stress–strain response matches the tension experiments of Barthelat & Espinosa (2007).

Why toughness peaks: a trade-off

More mortar means the aragonite tablets carry less strain, so they crack later and the composite stretches further (area C below). But the composite is also weaker, so the curve sits lower (area B is lost). Toughness rises while the gain from C beats the loss from B. Past about 20% mortar (30% for the stiffer matrix), the loss wins. With enough mortar the tablets never reach 160 MPa at all, and the composite stops failing in a brittle way.

Takeaways

  • MD puts the shear modulus of nacre's organic matrix (Lustrin A + Perlucin) at 1.25–1.45 GPa, narrowing a 100× spread in the literature.
  • That value, used directly in a 3D brick-and-mortar FE model, reproduces measured elastic behaviour without fitting.
  • 10–20% organic content gives the highest toughness. Adding more mortar always costs stiffness and strength.
  • A softer matrix (~3.8 GPa) gives the highest optimum toughness, but only at the right fraction; at 5% it is worse than a stiff one.
  • Next steps: add mineral bridges and nano-asperities so the model captures nacre's plastic regime.
